# Five Cyber Hygiene Habits Every Student Should Practise

Your phone, laptop and online accounts hold more than entertainment. They may contain class notes, assignments, photos, personal messages and access to important services. Protecting them does not require becoming a cybersecurity expert. A few consistent habits can reduce avoidable risks.

## 1. Update your devices through trusted channels
Software updates often include security fixes. Delaying them for too long can leave known weaknesses unpatched. Turn on automatic updates when suitable, and check your operating system, browser and frequently used apps. Install updates through the device's settings or the publisher's official store. Do not trust an unexpected pop-up simply because it says your device is infected or out of date. Close the message and check for updates independently.

## 2. Back up important work—and test it
Imagine losing your laptop the night before an assignment deadline. A backup can make the difference between recovering your work and starting again. Keep important files in a suitable second location, such as a reputable cloud backup service or an external drive. For important data, avoid relying on a single copy that is always connected to the same device. Check occasionally that the files can actually be restored.

Remember that synchronisation and backup are not always the same. A synchronised folder may also synchronise deletions or unwanted changes. Understand what your chosen service retains and how its recovery features work.

## 3. Question app permissions
A flashlight app usually does not need your contacts. A photo-editing app may need access to selected photos, but that does not mean it needs continuous location access. When an app requests a permission, ask whether the feature you are using genuinely requires it. Choose limited or one-time access when your device offers that option. Review permissions periodically in your settings; the exact menu depends on your device and operating-system version.

## 4. Be careful on public Wi-Fi
Free Wi-Fi at a café, station or campus can be useful, but you may not know who operates the network or who else is connected. Confirm the network name with the venue instead of choosing a similar-looking name. Keep your device updated and use websites that protect the connection with HTTPS. Avoid sensitive transactions if you cannot establish that the network and website are trustworthy. A reputable VPN may add protection for network traffic, but it does not make a fraudulent website safe.

## 5. Check links before you open them
A message may look as if it came from a delivery company, bank, college or friend. Before opening a link, consider whether you expected it and inspect the actual domain. Be suspicious of urgent demands, unexpected prizes and requests to sign in through a link. For important services, open the official app or type a known address yourself.

The padlock or HTTPS indicator is useful, but it is not a trust certificate. It means the connection to that domain is encrypted; a deceptive website can also use HTTPS. Check the domain and the context.

## Make the habits routine
Choose one small action today: enable updates, review app permissions, back up an assignment folder or remove an unknown saved Wi-Fi network. Repeat the check periodically. Cyber hygiene is not a one-time installation or a guarantee against every attack. It is a set of practical habits that make your digital life more resilient.

**Remember:** Update safely. Keep recoverable backups. Limit permissions. Treat public networks carefully. Check links and domains.
